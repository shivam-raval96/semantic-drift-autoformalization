Informalizing ETP · Experiment 12

Majority voting at the frontier

The 20 hardest story pairs in the corpus — both laws carrying ten operations each — formalized by Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5, ten times per temperature arm, with answers pooled by grading-equivalence class and settled by majority vote. Vote@k is measured for every k from 1 to 10, against a temperature-0 single pass.

Grok 4.3 ran in the same experiment and is omitted from every figure: 0 of 20 correct in all 21 runs — nothing for a vote to amplify.

Figure 1

Accuracy climbs the vote ladder, then saturates

Correct rate over the 20 pairs as ballots accrue, one panel per model. The gray rule is the temp-0 single pass. Opus 4.8 reaches 100% by vote@4 in both arms and never leaves it. GPT-5.5 gains slowly at t 0.7 and not at all at t 1.0 — an expected split: GPT-5.5 ignores the temperature parameter, so its two arms are one distribution sampled twice.

Figure 3

Agreement is calibration — with one telling exception

Each dot is one (pair, arm) cell at k = 10, placed by how many of its ten ballots the winning answer received; both arms pooled. Consensus splits the cells almost perfectly: none of the at 0–1 ballots yields a correct answer (the all-abstain ones yield no answer at all), while the at ≥6 ballots are all correct but one. The exception is GPT-5.5's S362→S338 at t 1.0 — six identical wrong ballots. A model whose samples don't vary can be confidently wrong; consensus is only evidence when the sampling is real.
