Formalizing informal mathematics — main results

Models recover an Equational Theories Project implication from natural language into a rigid grammar (ASSUME: / ASK:), graded syntactically. Fuzzy language is a themed Storyform narrative; abstracted language is a structured literal description of the same pair. Vacuous laws (E1/E2) are excluded.

1–2 · Formalization requires thinking

With thinking on, open-weight and closed models look alike — seven of eight sit at 93–100% on the same 30 stories. With thinking off, the field spreads from 0% to 57%, and the frontier stays high: GPT-5.5 scores 92% on stories with reasoning fully off (exp 06/07), where smaller models fall into the teens and thirties.

3–4 · Abstracted language, and a two-stage pipeline

Models are far better at formalizing abstracted natural language than fuzzy narrative when they cannot think. Routing the story through an explicit abstraction step, then formalizing that description, captures most of that gap: two-stage matches the literal arm and beats one-shot story by ~16 mean points. Prompting the model to “abstract first” in a single call does not (exp 08: +0.5 / −3.2).

5 · Majority voting does not improve formalization

K-sample self-consistency on the hard story arm (temperature 0.7, no thinking) does not recover accuracy. Four open-weight models move from 26.1% at a single greedy pass to 27.8% at vote@7 — a +1.7 point change at 7× cost. Errors are stable misreadings, not sampling noise around a correct modal answer.
