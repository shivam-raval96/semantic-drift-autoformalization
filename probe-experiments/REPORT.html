Probing and steering: does the model know internally when a formalization is correct?

Harsh Soni · probe-experiments, branch harsh/experiments · August 2026

Experiment

Models are weak when you ask them "is this formalization correct, yes or no". This experiment tests whether the answer exists inside the model anyway. The model reads a story plus a candidate rigid-grammar answer, and a linear probe is trained on its hidden states to separate correct from wrong answers. Probes are always scored on equations from held-out law families (law-disjoint splits), so nothing can be memorized: if the probe works, the model carries a general internal notion of correctness, whether or not it can say it. Then two follow-ups: where does that knowledge sit relative to the model's yes/no answering machinery, and does the model causally use it.

Data

contrast_v1: 1,000 problems (easy / medium / hard thirds). Each yields two texts: the reference rigid grammar (correct) and the same grammar with ONE small AST edit that changes the meaning (wrong), so both look equally like valid notation and only semantics separates them. Edits that fall inside the grader's symmetry group were rejected and redone. 2,000 texts, exact 50/50, every label mechanical (checkform), frozen and sha-pinned.

Two reads. First, every model above the 8B carries a real correctness signal on laws it never saw (the lexical floor under the same split is 0.503). Second, capability does not fully explain representation: Qwen3.5-4B and Llama-3.3-70B behave identically on the question (0.626 vs 0.629) yet the 70B's internal signal is far stronger (0.549 vs 0.634, CIs non-overlapping). Scale strengthens what the model knows even when behavior looks the same.

Result 2: the knowledge is stored silently, and the model never uses it to answer

Mechanism work on Qwen3-32B. Three independent tests, same conclusion:

  • Geometry. The probe's correctness direction is orthogonal to the model's yes/no verbalization axis at every one of the 63 testable layers (max |cosine| 0.019, below the random baseline's p95 of 0.027). Replicated on Qwen3.6-27B with two independent lens methods (J-lens and R-lens): 0 of 63 layers exceed random for either.
  • Vocabulary. Pushed through the unembedding, the direction maps to no meaningful tokens (top score 0.114 vs 0.112 for a random direction; the positive control reading the actual yes/no axis scores 0.950 on literal "yes" tokens).
  • Causality. Injecting the direction into the forward pass does nothing, on a harness where injection demonstrably works:

Result 3: asking the question is what routes the knowledge into the verbal channel

Figure 2. How well the model's own yes/no axis separates correct from wrong, by layer. Blue: the real verification question. Orange: a placebo question at the same token position. Dashed: no question at all.

With the real question, the yes/no axis becomes correctness-predictive at layers ~48–57 (peak 0.689). With a placebo question at the exact same position it stays at chance there (0.483, max gap +0.212); the placebo only drifts up in the last few layers, which is a generic answer-position effect. So the correctness information exists regardless (Result 1), but it gets connected to the answering machinery only when the question demands it, and only late in the network.

What this means

  • Knowing and saying are decoupled: the model computes correctness internally but stores it outside its verbal channel, and never consults it when answering unless asked.
  • Scale grows the internal signal beyond what behavior shows (the 4B vs 70B comparison), so behavioral evals underestimate what larger models represent.
  • Ties to the grammar-FT result: fine-tuning changed how the model writes but the internal correctness signal was untouched (law-disjoint probe 0.52–0.53 before and after FT).

Checks behind these numbers

Law-disjoint splits everywhere; lexical floor recomputed under the same split; shuffled-label and layer-0 controls clean; placebo question and position-matched reads for Result 3; norm-matched random and negated vectors for steering; bootstrap CIs and permutation nulls on every headline number; Result 2 replicated on a second model with two independent lens methods. Earlier claims that failed these checks were retracted and are logged with their post-mortems in RESEARCH_LOG.md.
